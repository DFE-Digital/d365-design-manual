Keep the business unit structure simple. Only create a business unit when a group of records needs to be kept apart from others for security or regulatory reasons. See how business units work with security roles for examples.

Do

  • Use business units to separate data with different security needs, such as restricted cases.
  • Keep the hierarchy as flat as possible.
  • Turn on record ownership across business units, and give users roles from the business units they need.
  • Use teams and security roles for access across business units, rather than moving users.
  • Use organisation-owned tables for data everyone shares, such as reference data.
  • Review the structure whenever a new service is onboarded or teams change.

Don't

  • Don't create a business unit for every team or directorate, or copy the organisation chart.
  • Don't move users between business units often. It changes what they can see, and can change who owns records.
  • Don't use business units to group data for reporting. Filter on the data instead.
  • Don't use separate business units for areas that don't need their data kept apart, such as different policy teams. Use one business unit, and control access with security roles and record ownership.

Remember that a user in the root business unit with organisation-level access can see records in every business unit, including restricted ones.
