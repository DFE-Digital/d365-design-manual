Business units, security roles and teams together decide what each user can see and do. This page explains how they combine, with examples of how DfE CRMs use them. Microsoft's security concepts in Dataverse (opens in a new tab) covers the detail.

How access works

  • Business units set boundaries for data. They form a hierarchy under a root business unit, and every user belongs to one.
  • Security roles set what a user can do with each table, such as read, create or update, and how far that reaches.
  • Teams pass their security roles on to their members, and can own records.

How far a permission reaches is its scope:

  • User: records the user owns
  • Business unit: records owned in the business unit
  • Parent: child business units: records owned in the business unit and the units below it
  • Organisation: all records

Examples of scope

These examples use this business unit structure.

  • A user in UK with business unit read access to cases sees cases owned in UK only.
  • A user in UK with parent: child read access to cases sees cases owned in UK, North and South, but not International.
  • A user in International with organisation read access to cases sees every case.
  • A user in International who joins a team in UK, where the team's role gives business unit access to cases, sees UK cases through the team without moving business unit.

Most users have a mix. For example, organisation access to contacts and organisations, so everyone works from the same records, and business unit access to the service's own cases.

How DfE CRMs use this

A service with its own business unit

This is the current set-up in several DfE CRMs:

  • The service has its own business unit. Its default team gives every user in it a basic role.
  • A service team in that business unit gives its members access to the service's tables and apps.
  • Small elevated roles are given directly to the few users who need them. For example, to publish a new version of the terms and conditions, replace documents users can download from the website, or open a new submission window.

This keeps most access on teams, and gives extra permissions to individuals only for specific tasks.

A restricted business unit for sensitive records

This CRM uses record ownership across business units:

  • General work sits in the root business unit, and sensitive work, such as fraud and safeguarding, sits in a restricted business unit below it.
  • Each area has owner teams that own its cases.
  • The owning business unit is mandatory on cases, so every case is deliberately placed in the general or restricted unit, whoever creates it.
  • Only users with a role from the restricted business unit can see the restricted cases.

At the moment every team has the same standard role. If teams later need different access, it can be added through each team's roles.

Record ownership across business units

Record ownership across business units is a setting you turn on for each environment in the Power Platform admin center. With it:

  • a user still belongs to one business unit, but can be given security roles from other business units
  • each role's scope applies to the business unit the role comes from, not the user's own
  • a user can own, or create, records in any business unit where one of their roles lets them read that table
  • every user still needs a basic role from their own business unit to use model-driven apps

This is the standard for DfE CRMs. Turn it on for new environments, and move existing environments to it as they're updated. Give users roles from the business units whose data they need, rather than moving them between business units. Make the owning business unit mandatory on tables where it separates sensitive data.

Key points

  • Business units decide where records sit. Security roles decide what users can do with them.
  • Without record ownership across business units, a role's scope applies to the user's own business unit, and moving a user changes what they can see.
  • With it, a role's scope applies to the business unit the role comes from.
  • Organisation scope gives access to every record in that table.
  • Teams give shared access without moving users.
