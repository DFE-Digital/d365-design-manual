Base security roles on what people need to do, not who they are or where they sit. Microsoft explains security roles and privileges (opens in a new tab).

Do

  • Create roles for job responsibilities, such as Case Worker, not for teams or individuals. For example, one Case Worker role for every policy area, with data access controlled by business units and teams.
  • Assign roles to teams where possible, rather than to users.
  • Give each role the least privilege it needs, and only use organisation-level access when it's essential.
  • Create small add-on roles for specific extra permissions, such as importing data or publishing content, and give them only to the users who need them.
  • Document what each role is for, especially roles for one service.
  • Review roles regularly to check they still match how the service works.

Don't

  • Don't create a new role for every small change.
  • Don't give users several overlapping roles without checking what they add up to.
  • Don't copy and rename a role without understanding what it contains.
  • Don't give users high privileges "just in case".
  • Don't use broad roles to work around a poor business unit design.

Too little access breaks forms, views and navigation, so test roles with real user accounts.
