Microsoft explains how to create and design forms (opens in a new tab), including the different form types. Follow these standards when you do.

Forms for different roles

  • Create a separate form when a user group's needs are significantly different, rather than one form with everything on it. For example, a triage form for contact centre agents and a full case form for case managers.
  • Use security roles to control which forms each group sees, and test with real user accounts to check the right form opens by default.

Layout

  • Put the most important columns at the top left, and columns needed for a new record near the top.
  • Use tabs for major areas, such as Details, History and Documents, and sections within them, rather than one long form.
  • Avoid empty space on the form.
  • Keep layouts consistent with the other forms in your CRM.

Related records

  • Use subgrids where users often need to see related records, and filter them to the records that matter.
  • Put large subgrids on their own tab, and don't put more than 5 or 6 subgrids on one tab, because they slow the form down.
  • Use quick view forms to show details from a related record without opening it.

Names

  • Name forms by table and audience, such as Case - Agent View or Application - Admin Form.
  • Give tabs and sections clear names, such as Address, History or Funding, not Tab_2 or Section_3.

Form logic

Keep business rules, scripts and controls on forms light, because they slow the form down. Use the simplest option that works. See JavaScript or business rules.
